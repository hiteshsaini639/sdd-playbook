# Replacing a paper sheet

People already fill in some paper forms: a checklist, an attendance
register, a scorecard. When software replaces one, they trust it if it
keeps what the paper did well and fixes what it did badly. Learned on a
shop's back office that moved its monthly paper sheets onto tablets.

- **Keep the paper's shape where people read it.** The month view is the
  paper's grid, and it prints one page per sheet, laid out like the
  original. Staff and inspectors already know how to read it.
- **Fix what the paper hides.** On paper, a blank box can mean "not done"
  or "nobody checked". On screen, each item is marked Done or Not done,
  and an item nobody marked is shown as unmarked.
- **Mark it during the day, not from memory at the month's end.** One tap
  on the phone or tablet in the shop, and it saves itself.
- **A sign-off replaces the signature.** The person responsible signs the
  month off, with remarks. That locks it, and the printout carries their
  name and the time.
- **A signed month never changes.** It is drawn from a copy taken at
  sign-off, so renaming or removing an item later does not rewrite it.
  Reopening it needs its own permission. The old sign-off stays in the
  history, and the month has to be signed again.
- **The owner edits the lists.** Each item has a start date and an end
  date, so an item added this month does not show as missed on the days
  before it existed.
- Keep scans of the originals in the repo's ignored private folder, and
  build the first lists from them.
- Let the first sheet set the pattern the others reuse: a list someone
  edits, a day to fill in, and a month to sign and print.
